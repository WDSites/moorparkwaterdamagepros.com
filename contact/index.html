Call the 24/7 dispatch line

Phone is the only way to start a job, and the line is answered around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays. A real person asks what happened, which rooms are wet and what you have already shut off. Same-day response is offered when crews are available.

If you smell gas or see sparks, leave the building and call 911 or your utility before calling us. If sewage is involved, keep people and pets out of the water until a crew has looked at it.

What to say when you call

A few details help the dispatcher plan, and none of them need to be exact.

  • Where the water came from, such as a pipe, appliance, roof, toilet or storm
  • Which rooms and floors are wet, and roughly how large the area is
  • What you have already done, such as shutting off the main valve or powering down wet areas
  • The flooring, ceiling and wall types you can see, such as carpet, hardwood or drywall
  • Whether you plan to contact your insurer, since photos and notes help

What happens after the call

The dispatcher tells you what to do while the crew is arranged. When the crew arrives, they check the source, map the moisture and explain the scope and price before any work starts. If the problem belongs to another trade, such as a plumber, a roofer or a waterproofing contractor, they tell you that too.

Quick safety checks before you call

If you can do it safely, shut off the main water valve to stop the source. If the water is touching outlets, appliances or a panel, do not stand in it, and leave power alone unless you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground. For any electrical or gas concern, call your utility or 911 first.

Move people and pets out of rooms with sewage or storm water. Lift small furniture and electronics off wet floors, and photograph the damage before moving anything else. These steps take a few minutes and they protect both your safety and your insurance documentation.

Then call. The dispatcher can talk you through the rest while a crew is arranged, and it is fine to call back if the situation changes.

Calling about a smaller problem

Not every call is an emergency. A ceiling stain that has been there a week, a musty closet or a floor that looks slightly cupped is worth a call too, and the dispatcher will treat it that way. Small problems are cheaper to fix when they are caught early, and a quick description is often enough to say whether a crew should look at it.

If you are not sure whether what you found is water damage, mold or an old stain, describe it anyway. The dispatcher asks about color, smell, location and whether it has changed, and can usually tell you which service fits. If the answer is that nothing needs doing yet, you will hear that too.

For insurance questions, your insurer is the right contact for coverage decisions. We can explain what documentation a crew usually provides, such as photos, moisture readings and a written scope, so you know what to ask your insurer to accept.

Visiting the office

The Moorpark address is for correspondence and records. Emergency work happens at your property, so please call before coming to the office. If you want to drop off documents for a job, ask the dispatcher whether that is needed, since most paperwork can be handled by phone and by photos from your own device.
