Where the dispatch line sends crews

Moorpark itself is the home base, with ZIP code 93021 covering the city. From there, crews travel to the four neighboring towns that most often need water damage help. Simi Valley sits to the east along State Route 118, Thousand Oaks lies to the south, Camarillo is southwest, and Fillmore is farther west up the Santa Clara River valley.

Because the towns are different places with different water suppliers, flood maps and housing, each has a dedicated page. Read the one that matches your address, then call the dispatch line and describe the problem. If your address is on the edge of a town, say so on the call, since service is decided by where the property is and not only by its ZIP code.

ZIP codes covered

Moorpark is served under ZIP code 93021. In Simi Valley the covered ZIP codes are 93063 and 93065, and in Thousand Oaks they are 91360 and 91362. Camarillo is served under 93010 and 93012, and the nearby community of Somis, ZIP code 93066, is dispatched from the Camarillo side. Fillmore is served under ZIP code 93015.

If your ZIP code is not on this list but you are in southern Ventura County, call anyway. The dispatcher will tell you honestly whether a crew can reach you.

Why local water details matter

A restoration plan depends on where the water came from and how the building was made. Moorpark gets its water from Ventura County Waterworks District No. 1, while Simi Valley and Thousand Oaks each have more than one water supplier, so the shutoff and meter locations are not always the same from one street to the next.

Flood exposure varies too. FEMA has revised flood maps along the Arroyo Simi in Moorpark and in Simi Valley, and the Santa Clara River shapes the risk in Fillmore. Find your address on the FEMA flood map and ask whether your policy covers flood, because most standard homeowners policies do not.

What to have ready when you call from another town

The dispatcher needs the street address, the nearest cross street and the town name, because several Ventura County communities share similar street names. Say which water supplier bills you if you know, since that helps when the problem begins at the meter. Mention any access issues such as a gated community, a steep driveway or a locked side gate.

If the water came from outside the building, such as a creek, a storm drain or a road, say so. That points toward a flood or storm response rather than a plumbing one, and the crew plans differently. Photos on your phone help, and the crew will ask for them later if your insurer wants documentation.

Choosing the right town page

Read the town page that matches your address for local detail on water suppliers, flood maps and housing. The Simi Valley page explains its two water suppliers and recent FEMA map changes, and the Thousand Oaks page covers hillside and creek drainage. The Camarillo page walks through the seasons, and the Fillmore page covers the river history that shapes risk there.

None of those pages replaces a call. They exist so you arrive at the phone with the right facts and a clear idea of what to ask.

When distance matters

Moorpark sits in the middle of this group, which keeps travel between the towns reasonable. Storm days change that, since flooded roads and closed highways can slow any crew, so tell the dispatcher about road conditions you can see. Honest information on both sides gets a crew to you sooner than a promise nobody can keep.

What to do before the crew arrives, in any town

Shut off the main water valve if a pipe is the source, keep people and pets out of the wet area, and do not walk through standing water near outlets. Photograph the damage before anything is moved. Call 911 or your utility for any electrical or gas concern before calling the dispatch line.
