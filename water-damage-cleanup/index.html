Signs the water is spreading and cleanup cannot wait

  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or a pad that stays dark after you blot it.
  • Drywall that feels cool, soft or swollen, or paint that bubbles at the bottom edge.
  • A sour, swampy or sewer smell that returns after you open the windows.
  • Water stains that grow overnight on a ceiling, or a ceiling that sags in the middle.

Any one of these means water has moved past what you can see. A a meter and a thermal camera find the wet edge, often a few feet beyond the stain.

How a cleanup runs, step by step

  1. Stop and survey

    The crew confirms the source is off, sets the water category, and maps wet areas with a moisture meter and thermal camera.

  2. Extract the water

    Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull standing water off carpet, tile and subfloor.

  3. Remove what cannot be saved

    Soaked pad, swollen drywall, wet insulation and ruined contents are cut out and bagged.

  4. Clean and sanitize

    Surfaces that stay are cleaned, then treated with antimicrobial applied per the product label.

  5. Deodorize and hand off

    Odor sources are removed and the job moves to drying.

What gets removed and what can be saved

Not every wet material is a loss. What the water carried, how long the material sat in it and whether it is porous decide the outcome. A hardwood floor under clean water may dry in place, but under sewage backup it is a removal job.

Category 3 is sewage or outside flood water. Category 2 is significantly contaminated, such as a washing machine discharge. Ask which category the crew is treating and why.

Cleaning, antimicrobial treatment and odor

Cleaning removes soil and residue. Antimicrobial treatment is a separate step, applied only where the water category or material calls for it and only as the product label allows. A crew that sprays everything by default is not following the label.

Odor usually means something wet is still in place. Sour smells from pad, a soaked baseboard cavity or damp insulation end when the material is removed, not when it is masked. If a smell returns, call back before the rebuild closes the wall.

Electrical, sewage and standing water: stay out first

Sewage backup and outside flood water are Category 3. They need protective gear, containment and removal of porous materials from the first minute.

Safety before cleanup

If water reached outlets, appliances or the panel, or if there is a gas smell, leave the area and call 911 or your utility before you call anyone else. Cut power only if the box is reachable from a dry spot. Keep people and pets out of sewage and flood water.
