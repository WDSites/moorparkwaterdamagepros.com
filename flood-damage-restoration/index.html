Floodwater Is Spreading: What To Do Right Now

If floodwater is coming into your Moorpark, CA home, treat it as urgent, and know that our 24/7 dispatch line is open. Do not wade through it. Floodwater can carry debris and contamination, and the water can be electrically charged if power lines or outlets are involved. If you can do it from dry ground, shut off the main water valve and lift valuables, then call.

Flood damage restoration is the removal of Category 2 and 3 water, the tear-out of materials that cannot be cleaned, cleaning and disinfection, and structural drying. It is more cautious than a clean-water job because the water itself is the hazard. Same-day response is available when a crew is open.

Stay out of moving water and away from electricity

The National Weather Service warns that six inches of fast-moving water can knock over an adult and 18 to 24 inches can carry away most vehicles, including large SUVs. Do not walk or drive through it. Do not touch electrical equipment if it is wet or if you are standing in water, and call 911 or the utility BEFORE calling a restoration crew if you smell gas or see downed lines.

Why Moorpark Homeowners Should Check the Flood Map

The City of Moorpark lists two FEMA Letters of Map Revision that changed the city's Flood Insurance Rate Maps. One is Case No. 10-09-2904P for the Arroyo Simi and the other is Case No. 12-09-0985P for the Walnut Canyon Drain. Maps get revised, so a zone you heard about years ago may not be current.

Whether you live in Old Town Moorpark north of SR 118 or in Peach Hill and Mountain Meadows south of the Arroyo Simi, the map for your parcel is what counts. Search your parcel on the FEMA flood map, or ask Moorpark City Hall at 805-517-6200. The FEMA Map Service Center is 1-800-358-9616. Your answer tells you what coverage to ask your insurer about.

Ventura County Public Works advises residents to learn how to get and place sandbags before floodwaters threaten. They can slow water at a door, but they do not replace pumping once water is inside.

Basement Flooding Cleanup: If Your Home Has a Basement, Lower Level or Sunken Garage

Not every Moorpark home has below-grade space, so this applies only if your home has a basement, lower level or sunken garage. In those spaces, water collects from storm runoff, groundwater or a failed sump, and even a few inches can wick into finished drywall, cabinetry and stored belongings within hours.

The American Red Cross advises pumping a flooded basement gradually, about one-third of the water per day, because draining it all at once can let soil pressure outside push on the walls. A crew uses submersible pumps, then extraction and drying, and removes porous items that touched the water such as carpet, mattresses and insulation.

Be clear on what this work is. Flood damage restoration cleans and dries what the water touched, but it is not waterproofing. If water keeps coming back, you will need a drainage or waterproofing specialist, and ask your city building department what rules apply to changes below grade.

Raw sewage is coming up my floor drain, what do I do?

Sewage in a shower, floor drain or toilet is not a plumbing annoyance. It is Category 3 water, and it needs a different response from a leak. Stay out of the room and keep children and pets away.

Do not use sinks, toilets or the washing machine, since that sends more water down the same line. The blockage may be in your lateral or in the public line, so call a plumber or your sewer provider as well and treat the cleanup as a separate job.

Sewage Backup Cleanup: Safety First, Emergency Steps

A sewage backup is an emergency, and our dispatch is open 24/7. Stay out of the affected area until a crew with protective gear arrives. Do not try to mop it up with household cleaners, because that spreads contamination and puts you in contact with it.

The crew contains the area, sets up negative air machines or HEPA air scrubbers when needed, and wears gloves, boots and respiratory protection. Standing sewage is extracted, and porous materials such as carpet, pad, drywall and insulation that touched it are removed and bagged. Hard surfaces are cleaned with an antimicrobial used as the product label directs, then the structure is dried.

Fixing the blockage is a plumbing job, not a restoration job. Your insurance may cover a backup only with a specific endorsement, so read the policy and confirm with your carrier before the cleanup bills arrive, and take photos before anything is removed.

Sewage safety

Do not touch sewage with bare hands, do not step in it and do not run fans or vacuums that spread it. If the backup has reached outlets or an appliance, cut power only if the panel is dry and call 911 or the utility BEFORE calling us.

Flood Damage Is Usually Not in a Standard Homeowners Policy

FEMA says homeowners and renters insurance do not typically cover flood damage, and Ventura County Public Works makes the same point. Flood coverage is a separate policy, usually through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private insurer. Most NFIP policies carry a 30-day waiting period, so buying after water arrives does not help that event.

That is why the flood map check matters before a storm. If you have flood coverage, call your insurer first, photograph everything, keep a log of calls, and make temporary repairs that stop further damage, as the California Department of Insurance advises. If you do not, ask your insurer what applies.
