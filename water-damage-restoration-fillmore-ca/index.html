Water damage restoration in Fillmore starts at the rivers

Water damage restoration in Fillmore, CA is shaped by geography first. The town was built at the confluence of the Sespe and the Santa Clara River, a spot picked in 1887 when the railroad arrived. Rivers gave Fillmore its site, and they are why flood water is a real question here and not a footnote.

Fillmore is in Ventura County, about 16 miles west of Moorpark, in ZIP 93015. Most calls start with a plumbing failure, but high water is the reason crews here plan for access, contamination and long drying jobs differently.

What the Sespe and the Santa Clara have done before

The Fillmore Historical Museum records that torrential rain washed out the Sespe and Santa Clara bridges in 1914 and left Fillmore isolated. In 1938 the north approach to the Bardsdale Bridge washed out. Three major storms from January through March 1969 failed the approaches to the Sespe auto bridge and the railroad bridge.

In 1978, after months of rain, debris dammed the Sespe auto bridge, and the museum says 5 to 8 feet of water flooded the Los Serenos tract on the west side. The museum adds that the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers then agreed to build the levee that stands today. A levee lowers risk but does not remove it. Pull up your parcel on the FEMA flood map and ask about flood coverage, which standard policies usually exclude.

If water is still spreading in your Fillmore home

Shut off the water

Close the fixture valve or the main valve at the meter. If the water is coming from outside, do not wade out to find a valve.

Power off only if the panel is dry

Shut off breakers serving wet rooms only if the box is reachable without wading. Otherwise stay out and call your electric utility or 911.

Lift valuables and furniture

Raise documents, electronics and photos to a higher shelf or upstairs. Pull rugs off wet floors if you can do it without stepping in dirty water.

Avoid river and sewage water

Water that came from a river, creek or drain is Category 3. Keep children and pets out and do not run a household vacuum on it.

The dam failure, Bardsdale and the roads that close

In March 1928 the St. Francis Dam failed and sent water down the Santa Clara River, a reminder that river corridors here can carry far more than local rain. For a homeowner, history is only useful as a prompt. Ask your insurer what is and is not covered and keep your policy where you can find it fast.

Access matters as much as water. The museum notes that in 2005 high water closed Highway 126 west of Fillmore for several days, and mud slides closed Highway 23 over Grimes Canyon. When roads close, equipment and crews may take longer to reach you. Plan on calling early, keep a phone charged and tell dispatch which routes you can see are open.

Central Avenue and the older part of town

The museum says Main Street burned in 1903 and Central Avenue became the main street, with Central paved in 1917. City Hall sits at 250 Central Avenue today. Buildings in older parts of any town often have older supply lines and drain stacks, so a pinhole leak in a wall or a failed cast iron drain is a realistic cause.

We cannot tell you the age or pipe material of a particular Fillmore house from here. If your home is older, ask a plumber to look at the lines after the water is dry. Restoration can dry and rebuild the wet area, but it does not replace plumbing.

Flood water safety

Fillmore city water and who to call

The City of Fillmore website lists a Water and Sewer department at 250 Central Avenue, with listed office hours of Monday through Thursday, 8 am to 4 pm. That means a night or weekend leak will not wait for a city counter. Learn where your own meter and main valve are now.

The City of Fillmore also takes part in the Fillmore and Piru Basins Groundwater Sustainability Agency, which lists the city as having water supply authority within its boundaries. Trade press says the city runs a water recycling plant that sends treated water to irrigate two public schools, Two Rivers Park and a downtown greenbelt. If you see runoff from public landscaping, call the city. Inside your property line, the leak is yours to stop.
