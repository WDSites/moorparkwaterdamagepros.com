Which service do you need first?

Water damage jobs run in a sequence, and the first call depends on what the room looks like right now. Standing water on the floor needs extraction before anything else can happen, while a wall that stays damp after a repaired leak needs drying. A stained ceiling with no active drip often needs repair, and a musty closet after an old leak may need mold work.

The table below matches what you see to the service that usually comes first. If you are unsure, call the 24/7 dispatch line and describe the room. The dispatcher can tell you what to do while a crew is arranged, and the crew confirms the scope and price before work starts.

How the services fit together

Most real jobs use more than one service. A burst pipe under a kitchen sink might need extraction on day one, drying over the next several days, removal of the wet cabinet base and flooring, and then a rebuild once the readings say the structure is dry. Treating these as one job with stages keeps the plan clear and the price easier to follow.

Mold is the exception, since it is a consequence rather than a stage. If a leak was dried quickly, mold may never become a question. If a leak sat unnoticed behind a wall or in a closet, the water source has to be fixed first and the mold work follows. In Moorpark, where long dry stretches hide slow roof and window leaks until the first heavy rain, that sequence is common.

What every job includes

Whichever service you need, the same ground rules apply. Work is done by licensed, insured crews, and the crew lays out scope and price on arrival. The dispatch line runs 24/7, and same-day response is possible when crews are available.

Ask any company, including us, which published standards its crews follow. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers water damage restoration and ANSI/IICRC S520 covers mold remediation. A direct answer is a good sign, and a vague one tells you something too.

What shapes the price of each service

Prices are set by the job, not by a menu, and the same factors show up on every service. Water category matters because sewage and flood water need more protection and removal than a clean supply line. The affected area matters because more wet square footage means more equipment and labor.

Materials and time matter too. Hardwood, plaster and insulation behave differently when soaked, and drying can take several days with equipment running. Rebuild scope is often the largest variable, since replacing flooring, cabinets and drywall depends on what had to be removed. The crew explains each factor in the scope before work starts, and your insurer may ask for the same documentation.
