Three ways water gets into a Simi Valley home

Water damage restoration in Simi Valley, CA usually begins with one of three problems. A supply line or water heater fails inside the house, a drain backs up, or storm runoff reaches the structure. Each one calls for a different response, and the first question we ask is where the water came from. Crews are dispatched from Moorpark, about 7 miles west, and dispatch runs around the clock.

Restoration means stopping the spread, pulling standing water with extraction units, then drying framing, subfloor and drywall with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers until a moisture meter reads dry. It is not plumbing, roofing or waterproofing. The broken part still needs a licensed trade, and we will say so plainly.

If water is still spreading in Simi Valley: your first steps

Stop the source

Close the main water valve where the line enters the house or at the meter box. If the leak is a single fixture, use the shutoff under the sink or behind the toilet.

Power down wet areas

Flip the breakers for wet rooms only when you can get to the box from a dry floor. If water is near outlets and you cannot reach the panel dry, call the electric utility first.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, electronics, books and boxes onto furniture or counters. Pull up area rugs, because wet dye and backing stain the floor underneath.

Keep people and pets clear

Water from a drain, toilet or outdoor flooding may be Category 3. Keep children and animals out of it and do not wade through it barefoot.

Call the right line

City-side main or street problem: call the City Public Works hotline. Damage inside the building: call our 24/7 line and describe where the water came from.

Which water is it? Sorting damage by category

The source decides the plan. Clean supply water that is dried fast is a different job from a backed-up sewer line, even if both leave the same puddle. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry standard that defines these categories, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Two water suppliers, one city

Two suppliers serve Simi Valley. City of Simi Valley and Ventura County Waterworks District No. 8 serves about 60 percent of the city, with the City Council acting as its board, and Golden State Water Company serves the remaining 40 percent. The main source for the District is Calleguas Municipal Water District, plus two wells in Tapo Canyon that feed the Tapo Canyon Water Treatment Plant.

That split matters when a line fails. The shutoff at your house works the same either way, but the company that owns the meter and the street main depends on your address. Keep a recent bill where you can find it, and note the supplier name before an emergency, not during one.

What the 2024 flood map change does and does not mean

A FEMA Letter of Map Revision took effect October 22, 2024. It revised flood hazard information for the Arroyo Simi Channel (from upstream of First Street to downstream of Royal Avenue), Dry Canyon Channel, Erringer Drain, Runkle Canyon Channel and Tapo Canyon Creek Channel. According to the City, 1,903 properties came out of the Special Flood Hazard Area and 53 were newly added.

Check the current FEMA flood map for your address using the National Flood Hazard Layer viewer, and ask your insurer or lender what it means for you. The City also notes that nearly 25 percent of FEMA flood claims come from outside the high-risk zone. Map status never stops a water heater from splitting or a toilet supply line from failing, so interior leaks stay your risk either way.

Citywide remapping continues separately. The City describes its floodplain remapping project as an estimated four-year effort that has studied detailed models for the Arroyo Simi, Bus Canyon, Dry Canyon Las Llajas, North Simi Drain, Sycamore Canyon and Tapo Canyon watersheds.

How we dry a Simi Valley house

  1. Find and stop

    Trace the source, close the valve, and map wet areas with a moisture meter plus an infrared camera.

  2. Extract

    Truck-mounted or portable extraction units remove standing water from hard floors, carpet and pad.

  3. Open and dry

    Flood cuts, pulled baseboard and lifted flooring let air movers and LGR dehumidifiers reach wet framing.

  4. Verify

    Daily readings continue until the materials reach their dry standard, then rebuild scope is discussed.

Insurance and your paper trail

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Read your policy and confirm details with your carrier.

The California Department of Insurance advises making temporary repairs to prevent more damage and keeping a log of calls and copies of correspondence. Photograph everything before it is moved.
