What we do

Moorpark Water Damage Pros is a dispatch line built around one idea. When water is on the floor, you should reach a person at any hour, describe the problem in plain words and be told what to do next. We take the call, ask the questions a restoration crew would ask and arrange insured restoration technicians who hold the proper license for water damage and mold problems in Moorpark and nearby towns.

We do not publish a roster of names or invented statistics, because the useful facts about a restoration company are simpler. The line is answered around the clock, and you hear the plan and the price basis before anyone starts work.

What we will not claim

We do not promise arrival times, because traffic, storms and crew availability make that dishonest. We do not promise an insurance outcome, because coverage decisions belong to your insurer and your policy. We do not promise that mold will never return, because mold follows moisture and only moisture control lasts.

We also say plainly what restoration is not. It is not plumbing repair, roofing or basement waterproofing. A good crew will tell you when the real fix belongs to another trade, and that honesty is part of what you should expect from anyone you call.

Standards we point homeowners toward

Two published standards shape the trade. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers professional water damage restoration, and ANSI/IICRC S520 covers professional mold remediation. We name them so you can ask any company, including ours, whether its crews work to them.

You can also check a contractor on the California Contractors State License Board website and ask your insurer what documentation it wants. Our service pages explain how those pieces fit together for each type of loss.

How a job runs from our side

When you call, the dispatcher writes down the address, the source of the water, the rooms affected and any safety concerns. If there is a hazard, such as water near electrical panels or sewage, the first advice is about staying safe. Then a crew is arranged, and you are told what to do in the meantime.

On site, the crew confirms the source, checks moisture with meters and explains the scope and price. After that the work proceeds in stages, from extraction through drying to repair, and you can ask for documentation for your insurer at any point. If a stage turns out to be unnecessary, say so and ask what the crew recommends instead.

Questions worth asking any restoration company

Before you agree to work, ask who will be on site and whether they are licensed and insured. Ask which published standards the crew follows, how moisture will be measured and how you will know when drying is finished. Ask for the scope in writing and for an explanation of how the price is set.

Ask what happens if more damage turns up behind a wall, and whether you will be told before the scope changes. Ask what documentation you will receive for your insurer. A company that answers these directly is easier to work with, and the answers belong in writing before the equipment goes in.

We encourage you to ask these questions of us too. The dispatcher can explain how our process handles each one, and the crew on site can go into detail when they see the damage.

Local knowledge that changes advice

Moorpark gets about 15.85 inches of rain a year, mostly in a short winter season, so roof and window leaks tend to show up after the first heavy storm. We tell callers to check ceilings and window corners then, because early stains cost less to fix. That is the kind of local detail we use, and it comes from the City of Moorpark rather than from guesswork.
